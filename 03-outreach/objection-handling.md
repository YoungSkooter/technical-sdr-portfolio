# Objection Handling

## Objection 1 — "We're already automated."

### Don't say

> "Our automation is better."

### Say

> "That's actually interesting. What parts of the workflow are still
> difficult to automate?"

### SDR Principle

An objection can contain information about the prospect's environment.

---

## Objection 2 — "We already have a SIEM."

### Don't say

> "Our platform is better than your SIEM."

### Say

> "Makes sense. I'm less interested in replacing the SIEM and more in
> understanding what happens after an alert is generated. How much
> investigation happens outside that workflow?"

---

## Objection 3 — "We're happy with our current tools."

### Response

> "Understood. What parts of the current workflow are working
> particularly well?"

Then:

> "And where, if anywhere, does the team still have to work around
> the tooling?"

---

## Objection 4 — "Not interested."

### Response

> "No problem. Before I let you go, is that because the workflow isn't
> a priority, or because you've already solved it internally?"

The objective is not to force the conversation.

It is to determine whether there is useful information to capture.

---

## Objection 5 — "Send me something."

### Response

> "Absolutely. Which area would be most relevant — alert
> investigation, cloud-security context, or security-to-engineering
> remediation?"

This converts a vague request into a qualification opportunity.
